Subject: Week Ending September 6 -- Instrumenting the Search

Greetings,

Strategic Overview

I built the measurement layer for the job search this week. It's a monitoring site over the application pipeline, and it shows what went out, to whom, when, and what came back. Every application carries its role type, tier, submission date, and response state, and response rates break out by tier and by role type. The applications from last Friday are inside the five to seven day response window, so replies land in the tracker as they arrive.

Active Project Pipeline

The monitoring site is built and reading live data from the submission tracker. It holds every application as its own record: company, role, role type, tier, the date it went out, and where it currently stands. On top of those records it aggregates, so I can pull response rate by tier, response rate by role type, how long each application has been sitting without an answer, and which follow-ups come due this week. The numbers move as replies arrive rather than waiting on me to update anything. The commits on the build are public in my GitHub history.

I joined two communities where working practitioners critique each other's projects. The first is the AI Engineer Discord, a chat forum used by people building production software with these tools, where members post work in progress and take it apart in public. The second is r/LocalLLaMA, a large open-source machine learning forum where people run these models on their own hardware and review each other's tooling. I posted one of my own projects in both and gave feedback back on other people's.

I went back to contacts I already have, one specific question each rather than a general note, and asked about teams that are hiring now. Alongside that I pushed more of my work into public view and added the links to my application materials.

I did the groundwork on two more items from last week's list. On the Seattle side I went through the local AI and technology event calendars and pulled out the groups that run on a regular monthly schedule rather than posting one-off announcements, sorted by which are practitioner nights and which are recruiting events, and noted how far ahead each one opens registration. On the coursework side I went through the University of Washington catalog and separated the artificial intelligence and machine learning offerings by format: full certificate programs, single non-degree courses, and the evening and part-time schedules, with the cost, the start date, and the weekly time commitment for each. Both are shortlists with dates on them now instead of open questions.

I also opened a new line this week and started tracking AI competitions. There is a steady supply of hackathons and hack-offs running with real prizes, and a portion of them assign you to a team rather than expecting you to bring one. I am collecting the ones with dates I can actually make.
